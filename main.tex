\section{Introduction}

In the current information-driven era, extracting actionable insights from the staggering volume of unstructured online news data has become an increasingly complex challenge [1]. Traditional news aggregators and Information Retrieval (IR) systems, such as Google News, often rely on keyword-based search and chronological clustering, which frequently fail to capture the deep relational context and causal links between events, entities, and locations [1, 2]. This lack of relational depth necessitates a laborisous manual effort for users to establish a coherent chain of events across multiple articles [3].

This research builds upon the architectural foundation established by the \textbf{News Analytics as a Service (NAaaS)} project [4]. The NAaaS framework successfully introduced a distributed system using \textbf{Apache Spark} and \textbf{Apache Kafka} to extract "focus time" and "focus location" from news articles, effectively distinguishing between the metadata of an article's publication and the actual spatiotemporal focus of its narrative [4-6]. While NAaaS enabled the visualization of news on a \textbf{Geographic Information System (GIS)}, it primarily focused on spatial distribution rather than interpreting the complex causal narratives within the text [7, 8].

To address these cognitive gaps, we evaluate the proposed \textbf{NewsNet GraphRAG} approach, an evolutionary successor to the NAaaS system [9]. NewsNet introduces a hybrid solution that integrates \textbf{Named Entity Recognition (NER)} with \textbf{Knowledge Graphs (KGs)} and \textbf{Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)} to dynamically construct a causal representation of news articles [10, 11]. By utilizing a \textbf{Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GraphRAG)} pipeline, the system traverses these knowledge graphs to provide Large Language Models (LLMs) with a cause-and-effect sequence of events, offering a "new sense of depth" to automated news analysis [12, 13].

The primary objective of this paper is to rigorously evaluate the efficacy of the NewsNet approach against contemporary state-of-the-art (SOTA) benchmarks. We utilize the \textbf{DRAGON (Dynamic RAG Benchmark On News)} framework, which facilitates a dynamic evaluation of RAG systems through Knowledge Graph-based question generation [14, 15]. By testing NewsNet across the four core question types identified in SOTA research—\textbf{simple, set, multi-hop, and conditional}—we aim to demonstrate how graph-based retrieval improves context-aware reasoning and reduces hallucinations compared to standard vector-search baseline RAG systems [16, 17]. Through this evaluation, we contribute to the advancement of explainable and relationally-aware news analytics [18].



\section{Introduction}

In the contemporary information landscape, the rapid expansion of online news media has fundamentally transformed how information is produced, accessed, and consumed \cite{Mosolygo2025}. Despite these advances, deriving coherent and actionable insights from large volumes of heterogeneous and unstructured news content remains a major challenge. This challenge is commonly framed as global sensemaking, which requires connecting dispersed pieces of information into consistent, interpretable narratives that reflect evolving real-world events \cite{Kim2025}. Conventional Information Retrieval (IR) systems and baseline Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approaches \cite{Lewis2020} are limited by a persistent retrieval bottleneck. In particular, standard vector-based RAG relies primarily on semantic similarity between isolated text segments and therefore fails to capture the complex relational, temporal, and causal dependencies that are distributed across large news corpora \cite{Kim2025, Mosolygo2025, BichTram2025}.

This limitation is especially critical in domains where timely and accurate interpretation of news directly informs strategic, business, and financial decision-making. Analysts are typically required to search across multiple sources to reconstruct the sequence of events, underlying causes, and downstream impacts associated with a single major development. Such manual exploration of disparate documents to establish causal chains and contextual dependencies is both time-consuming and misaligned with the capabilities expected in modern Artificial Intelligence–driven analytical systems \cite{Haque2025}. Moreover, the absence of explicit relational structure in prevailing retrieval pipelines exacerbates the disconnect between user intent and news relevance, particularly when narratives span multiple articles, actors, and time periods \cite{Kim2025}.

To address these challenges, we propose NewsNet, a hybrid Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GraphRAG) framework for large-scale news analytics. By tightly integrating Knowledge Graphs (KGs) with Large Language Models (LLMs), NewsNet enables multi-hop reasoning and temporal analysis that go beyond the representational capacity of purely vector-based retrieval mechanisms \cite{BichTram2025, Mosolygo2025}. The proposed framework is designed to bridge the gap between fragmented reporting and holistic situational understanding through the following core components:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{NER-Driven Knowledge Graph Construction:} The framework automatically extracts entities and relations from unstructured news articles and incrementally constructs a contextualized entity–relation graph that preserves cross-document dependencies \cite{Kim2025, BichTram2025}.
\item \textbf{Transformer-Based Semantic Embeddings:} State-of-the-art transformer models are employed to generate dense sentence representations, enabling NewsNet to capture deeper semantic associations than those attainable through traditional lexical or keyword-based retrieval \cite{Mosolygo2025, Myers2025}.
\item \textbf{Dynamic Event Tracing:} By leveraging FAISS and scalable RAG architectures, NewsNet supports real-time retrieval and tracing of relevant events, facilitating continuous situational awareness across evolving news streams \cite{Mosolygo2025, Haque2025}.
\item \textbf{Community Detection and Synthesis:} The framework applies graph clustering techniques, including the \textbf{Leiden Community Detection} algorithm, to organize related entities and events into hierarchical communities \cite{Kim2025, Edge2025}. These communities enable the construction of coherent timelines and synthesized summaries, while ensuring scalability for real-world, large-scale deployments.
\end{itemize}

By transforming isolated news articles into an interconnected and interpretable network of entities, events, and relationships, NewsNet supports more transparent, comprehensive, and explainable global sensemaking. This capability directly enhances newsroom intelligence and high-stakes decision support, enabling analysts to move from fragmented information retrieval toward structured, context-aware understanding of complex and evolving news narratives \cite{Mosolygo2025, Jimenez2025}.
